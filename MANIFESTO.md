# Framio: a manifesto

> *Art belongs on the walls of the living.*

## Where it began

Framio started with a small, stubborn want: beautiful art on my own
television. The Samsung Frame turns a screen into a canvas — but filling it
meant hunting for high-resolution public-domain images, cropping them by hand
to 16:9, and losing quality along the way. So I built the tool I wished
existed.

## What it became

It grew past the one screen. What started as a way to dress a TV became a
living, interactive way to make art part of a day — on the television, yes,
but also the phone in your pocket, the tablet on the couch, the desk you work
at. Browsing became wandering. Collections became personal exhibitions.
Framing became something you do for whichever screen you'll actually live
with the piece on.

Framio is not a wallpaper manager. It is a quiet way to keep art close.

## Credit is the deal

We never take credit for other people's work — not the artists', not the
museums'. Every work names its maker wherever it appears. And every file
Framio exports carries the artist, the title, the museum, and the license
**embedded inside it**, so the credit travels with the image no matter where
it ends up. Crediting the people and institutions who made and kept this art
is not a footnote. It is the price of admission, and we pay it gladly.

## Open access is art's natural state

Framio works only with art that is genuinely open — public domain or CC0 —
released through museums' own open-access programs. Work that the public
funded, and that time has returned to everyone, belongs to everyone. We show
what is free to share, credit it plainly, and link back to the institution
that opened it. Works still in copyright are surfaced for discovery, never
for download.

## Access is the point

Accessibility is not a checklist at the end. It is a reason Framio exists.

Art that can only be *seen* is only half-open. So Framio treats descriptions
as first-class: open, community-written, CC0 descriptions that let someone who
cannot see an image still meet the work — read aloud on the artwork page, on
the TV, and carried inside every export. And it treats every way of moving
through the app as equal: a remote on the sofa, a finger on glass, a cursor
and keyboard at a desk. If a person cannot experience the art, the museum
isn't really open yet. We'd like to help close that gap.

## An invitation to institutions

If you are a museum, a gallery, an archive: open your collections, and we will
build you a shelf that credits you on every wall it reaches. There are simple
ways in — an existing open API, a small static file you publish, or an adapter
you or we write. See [docs/OPEN-ACCESS.md](docs/OPEN-ACCESS.md). The more of
the world's art that is open, the more of it can live in people's lives.

## An invitation to you

Framio is open source under the MIT license. Fork it, build on it, run your
own. Contribute a description so a work can be experienced without being seen.
Add a museum. Fix a bug. And tell me what you think — the good and the
graceless — at **pedrobritx@gmail.com**.

— Pedro Brito
